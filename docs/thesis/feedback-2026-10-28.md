## What the old budgets mean

The frozen pilot used additional LLZ squared error divided by the original
full-precision update's squared norm. Its eight saved training updates gave
p=1 lower quartile 5.923659, rounded down to 5.9, and p=2 upper quartile
6.928620, rounded up to 7.0. The latter also exceeds 1.1 times the tight budget.
These are squared-norm ratios, not 5.9% and 7%. Their corresponding allowed
extra-error norm multiples are sqrt(5.9)=2.429 and sqrt(7)=2.646.

The rule was frozen before new validation outcomes, which prevents retrospective
budget tuning within that pilot. It is nevertheless a heuristic for two
operating points, not a learning-preservation argument. The exact arbitrary
elements are the quartiles, rounding and 1.1 separation factor. Better explaining
the arithmetic does not by itself justify the budget scientifically.

Retain the original results and frozen protocol unchanged. Mark those values
as exploratory calibration outcomes, not recommended universal settings.

## Proposed independent budget-selection approach

1. **Define success before selecting a budget.** My proposed practical target
   for a future study is at most 1 percentage point loss in selected validation
   accuracy versus source-matched p=0, while lowering complete training-upload
   bytes. This is a proposed application tolerance, not a paper-derived constant,
   statistical equivalence margin already validated, or criterion of the old
   pilot. Report total bytes and computation as secondary costs. Freeze the
   final criterion before future validation results.
2. **Use the training-only rate-distortion evidence.** Inspect actual candidate
   bytes, extra error, total error and selected-p coverage. Candidate-error
   thresholds identify where a different p could become feasible on those
   particular updates. Keep the list small and do not assume such local
   counterfactuals predict a new training trajectory or validation accuracy.
3. **Make the units interpretable.** Report both the squared ratio tau and its
   square root, as well as error relative to the QSGD reconstruction error.
   Changing normalization creates a different policy; it must not be presented
   as making the existing reconstruction error smaller.
4. **Validate an independently specified rule.** A future bounded study should
   freeze candidate policies, a calibration/validation separation, controls,
   round count and seeds before training. Confirmation data must not be the
   same evidence used to choose the policy. Preserve the held-out test split.
   No new experiment batch is launched by this proposal.
5. **Select by the full objective and retain a fallback.** Prefer the feasible
   policy with lower uploads, subject to the predeclared learning criterion.
   If no lossy candidate meets it, retain p=0. A finding of no acceptable
   lossy budget is scientifically valid. Separate empirical acceptance from
   the sufficient budget conditions required by a theorem.

The practical 1 pp target above is my initial proposal to motivate a decision,
not a request that the supervisor choose the budget. Its suitability must be
explained in terms of the intended task. It can be revised before a future
protocol is frozen, not after that study's validation outcomes.
