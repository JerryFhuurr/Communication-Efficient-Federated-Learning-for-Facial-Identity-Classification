## 1–3 minutes: Main results

“In the current matched comparison, all methods use the same starting model
and data split for 15 rounds.”

| Method | Selected validation accuracy | Training upload MB |
|---|---:|---:|
| Uncompressed | 83.3% | 2028.759 |
| QSGD | 83.4% | 506.835 |
| QSGD + LLZ p=0 | 83.4% | 59.749 |
| QSGD + LLZ p=1 | 78.7% | 12.483 |

“p=0 reproduces the QSGD trajectory exactly and reduces its training uploads
by 88.21%. Total serialized bytes fall by 9.79%, because model downloads remain
uncompressed. These measurements include message metadata but are not actual
network captures.

p=1 compresses further, but loses 4.7 accuracy percentage points compared with
p=0. The additional saving in total bytes is only 1.15%. That makes p=0 our
current reference and p=1 a trade-off to investigate.”

## 3–4 minutes: What the results do and do not establish

“The stronger-model comparison currently uses one seed. The held-out test set
has not been evaluated, and we have not measured codec timing yet.

Our setup also differs from the paper's main experiments, including the absence
of error feedback. Therefore, the p=1 result does not show that the method is
generally unsuitable. I would like to investigate the cause through controlled
comparisons rather than assume that further model tuning will fix it.”

## 4–5 minutes: Proposed direction

“I propose keeping the model fixed, adding encoding/decoding and round timing,
and confirming the reference comparison with seeds 43 and 44. After that,
error feedback is a candidate for investigating the lossy accuracy trade-off.

I would like your advice on the expected thesis contribution and which future
extension should take priority.”
